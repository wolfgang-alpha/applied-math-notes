The Galerkin method

From the operator equation to a finite element solution in FEniCS

Lecture 1: the Galerkin method. Starting point is the operator equation Au = f; we derive the integral form and the variational formulation and solve three 1D heat conduction problems with FEniCS. The picture: a piecewise linear finite element function u_h = sum U_i phi_i, one hat function highlighted.

Theory

Inner product and operator equation

Inner product

of two functions on the domain $\Omega$

$$(f, g) = \int_\Omega f\, g \; dV$$

Operator equation

a compact way to write a differential equation

$$A\, u = f, \qquad u \in D(A)$$
  • $A$ contains all operations applied to the unknown function $u = u(x)$, for example $A = -\frac{d^2}{dx^2} + 1$.
  • The order $O(A)$ is the degree of the highest derivative in $A$. We write $O(A) = 2s$, where $s$ is the number of integrations by parts that can be applied to the operator.
The inner product of two functions is defined as (f, g) = integral of f g over Omega. Differential equations can be written more compactly by introducing an operator equation A u = f, u in D(A). The operator A contains all the operations applied to the unknown function u = u(x). The order of an operator is the degree of the highest derivative that appears in the operator. In connection with the order, the quantity s is introduced with O(A) = 2s: s is the number of integrations by parts that can be applied to the operator. Example: A = -d^2/dx^2 + 1 has O(A) = 2, so s = 1.

Theory

Two kinds of boundary conditions

The order of the operator decides the kind. With $O(A) = 2s$ we introduce $\; m = s - 1 .$

Essential or Dirichlet

The order of the derivative prescribed on the boundary is at most $m$:

$$\frac{d^{(c)} u}{dx^{(c)}} \text{ prescribed on } \partial\Omega, \qquad c \le m$$

In FEniCS: imposed on the function space with DirichletBC.

Natural or Neumann

The order of the derivative prescribed on the boundary is greater than $m$:

$$\frac{d^{(c)} u}{dx^{(c)}} \text{ prescribed on } \partial\Omega, \qquad c > m$$

In FEniCS: a boundary integral ... * ds in the linear form.

Depending on the operator of the differential equation, two kinds of boundary conditions can be distinguished, for which the additional quantity m = s - 1 is introduced. Essential or Dirichlet: the order of the derivative of the prescribed boundary values is less than or equal to m. Natural or Neumann: the order is greater than m. For a second-order operator (s = 1, m = 0): u itself is Dirichlet, the first derivative is Neumann.

Theory

Integral form of a differential equation

Multiply $Au = f$ by a test function $v$ and integrate over the domain $\Omega$:

$$\int_\Omega Au\; v \; dV = \int_\Omega f\; v \; dV \qquad \text{or} \qquad (Au, v) = (f, v)$$

Dirichlet boundary
where $u$ is prescribed: $\;v = 0$

Neumann boundary
$v$ is not subject to any condition

$v$ is an arbitrary continuously differentiable function. Every solution of the operator equation also solves the integral form, for every choice of $v$.

Consider the general operator equation Au = f. Multiplying both sides by a test function v and integrating over the domain Omega yields the integral form (Au, v) = (f, v). The test function v is an arbitrary continuously differentiable function that vanishes on the Dirichlet boundary, i.e. where u is prescribed. On the Neumann boundary v is not subject to any condition. Every solution of the operator equation is also a solution of the integral form for any choice of v.

Theory

Variational formulation

Integrate the integral form by parts $s$ times and incorporate the boundary conditions:

$$\underbrace{(Au,\, v)}_{2s \text{ derivatives on } u} \quad \xrightarrow[\ \text{insert boundary conditions}\ ]{\ \text{integrate by parts } s \text{ times}\ } \quad \underbrace{a(u,\, v)}_{s \text{ on } u, \; s \text{ on } v} \; = \; L(v)$$
  • $s$ derivatives move from $u$ onto the test function $v$.
  • Boundary terms appear. They vanish where $v = 0$ (Dirichlet boundary); on the Neumann boundary the prescribed data is inserted.
The variational formulation is obtained from the integral form (Au, v) = (f, v) by integrating by parts s times and incorporating the boundary conditions. In doing so, s derivatives are moved from u onto the test function v. a(u, v) collects the terms with u, L(v) the known terms.

Theory

Symmetry, positivity and linearity of an operator

Symmetric

$$(Au, v) = (Av, u)$$

for all $u, v \in D(A)$

Positive

$$(Au, u) > 0 \quad \text{for } u \neq 0$$

and $(Au, u) = 0$ for $u = 0$, for $u \in D(A)$

Linear

$$A(u - v) = Au - Av$$

 

An operator is symmetric if (Au, v) = (Av, u) for all u, v in D(A). It is positive if (Au, u) = 0 for u = 0 and (Au, u) > 0 for u != 0, u in D(A). It is linear if A(u - v) = Au - Av. These properties will be checked for the Laplace operator on the next slides.

Example

Heat conduction: $\;-\nabla \cdot \nabla u = f\;$ in $\Omega$

Operator

$$A = -\nabla \cdot \nabla$$ $$O(A) = 2, \quad s = 1, \quad m = 0$$

Dirichlet  $c = 0 \le m$
$u$ is prescribed on the boundary

Neumann  $c = 1 > m$
the normal derivative $\nabla u \cdot n$ is prescribed ($n$: outward normal vector)

Integral form

$$(Au, v) = -\int_\Omega (\nabla \cdot \nabla u)\, v \; dx = \int_\Omega f\, v \; dx = (f, v)$$
Operator A = -div grad, hence O(A) = 2, s = 1, m = 0. Dirichlet boundary condition (c = 0 <= m): u is prescribed on the boundary. Neumann boundary condition (c = 1 > m): the normal derivative grad u . n is prescribed (n is the outward normal).

Example

Integration by parts, once since $s = 1$

Gauss's divergence theorem:

$$-\int_\Omega (\nabla \cdot \nabla u)\, v \; dx = \int_\Omega \nabla u \cdot \nabla v \; dx - \int_{\partial\Omega} (\nabla u \cdot n)\, v \; ds$$

The boundary term vanishes where $v = 0$ (Dirichlet boundary) or where $\nabla u \cdot n = 0$ (homogeneous Neumann boundary).

Symmetric

$$(Au, v) = \int_\Omega \nabla u \cdot \nabla v \; dx = (Av, u)$$

Positive

$$(Au, u) = \int_\Omega |\nabla u|^2 \; dx \ge 0$$

for $u, v \in D(A)$ with homogeneous boundary conditions, so that the boundary term vanishes

Integration by parts once, since s = 1 (Gauss's divergence theorem). The boundary term vanishes where v = 0 (Dirichlet boundary) or where grad u . n = 0 (homogeneous Neumann boundary). For u, v in D(A) with homogeneous boundary conditions the boundary term vanishes, and (Au, v) = int grad u . grad v dx = (Av, u) as well as (Au, u) = int |grad u|^2 dx >= 0.

Example

Variational formulation with a Neumann boundary $\Gamma$

On $\Gamma \subset \partial\Omega$ the outward heat flux is prescribed, $q \cdot n = g$ with $q = -\nabla u$ (Fourier's law, conductivity 1). On $\partial\Omega \setminus \Gamma$, $u$ is prescribed and $v = 0$. Find $u$ such that

$$\int_\Omega \nabla u \cdot \nabla v \; dx = \int_\Omega f\, v \; dx - \int_\Gamma g\, v \; ds \qquad \text{for all } v .$$
$a(u, v)$
$L(v)$ · source
$L(v)$ · Neumann boundary
$\displaystyle\int_\Omega \nabla u \cdot \nabla v \; dx$
$\displaystyle\int_\Omega f\, v \; dx$
$\displaystyle -\int_\Gamma g\, v \; ds$
inner(grad(u), grad(v)) * dx
f * v * dx
- g * v * ds

Exactly this form is used in the FEniCS examples: the left-hand side in examples 1 to 3, the boundary term in example 3.

On the part Gamma of the boundary let the outward heat flux be prescribed, q . n = g with q = -grad u (Fourier's law, conductivity 1). On the remaining boundary u is prescribed, and there v = 0. Each term of the variational formulation becomes one term of the UFL forms a and L. In 1D, ds is the whole boundary, i.e. both end points; at the Dirichlet end the term drops out because v = 0 there.

FEniCS

Shared setup: mesh, function space, boundary markers


from fenics import *

tol = 1e-3
A   = 0.0
B   = 1.0
nr_elements = 20

mesh = IntervalMesh(nr_elements, A, B)
s    = FiniteElement('Lagrange', mesh.ufl_cell(), 1)
V    = FunctionSpace(mesh, s)

def left(x, on_boundary):
    return on_boundary and near(x[0], A, tol)

def right(x, on_boundary):
    return on_boundary and near(x[0], B, tol)

All three examples solve a 1D heat conduction problem on $\Omega = (0, 1)$ with piecewise linear Lagrange elements, $-u'' = f$, and differ only in $f$ and the boundary conditions.

The mesh, the function space and the two boundary markers are shared by all examples. near(x[0], A, tol) picks the boundary point at x = A; on_boundary is True only for boundary points.

FEniCS example 1

Dirichlet – Dirichlet

Temperature fixed at both ends, no source term:

$$-u'' = 0, \qquad u(0) = 0, \quad u(1) = 1$$ 

bc0 = DirichletBC(V, Constant(0.0), left)
bc1 = DirichletBC(V, Constant(1.0), right)

u = TrialFunction(V)
v = TestFunction(V)
f = Constant(0.0)

a = inner(grad(u), grad(v)) * dx
L = f * v * dx

u = Function(V)
solve(a == L, u, [bc0, bc1])
plot(u)
Temperature fixed at both ends: u(0) = 0, u(1) = 1, no source term (f = 0). Both boundary conditions are essential and are imposed on the function space through DirichletBC. The boundary term of the weak form vanishes because the test function is zero at both ends, so a(u, v) = int u' v' dx and L(v) = int f v dx. The solution is the straight line u = x.

FEniCS example 2

Dirichlet – homogeneous Neumann

Temperature fixed on the left, constant heat source, insulated right end:

$$-u'' = 1, \qquad u(0) = 0, \quad u'(1) = 0$$ 

bc = DirichletBC(V, Constant(0.0), left)

u = TrialFunction(V)
v = TestFunction(V)
f = Constant(1.0)

a = inner(grad(u), grad(v)) * dx
L = f * v * dx

u = Function(V)
solve(a == L, u, [bc])
plot(u)

Highlighted: the changes with respect to example 1. The right end gets no condition in the code at all.

Temperature fixed on the left, u(0) = 0; constant heat source f = 1. On the right end the temperature is not prescribed. In the weak form the boundary term int u' v ds is simply dropped, which corresponds to the natural boundary condition u'(1) = 0 (insulated end). The solution is the parabola u = x - x^2/2 with horizontal tangent at x = 1.

FEniCS example 3

Dirichlet – Neumann

Temperature fixed on the left, no source, flux $g = -1$ prescribed on the right:

$$-u'' = 0, \qquad u(0) = 0, \quad -u'(1) = g = -1$$ 

bc = DirichletBC(V, Constant(0.0), left)

u = TrialFunction(V)
v = TestFunction(V)
f = Constant(0.0)
g = Constant(-1.0)

a = inner(grad(u), grad(v)) * dx
L = f * v * dx - g * v * ds

u = Function(V)
solve(a == L, u, [bc])
plot(u)

Highlighted: the Neumann data enters only through the boundary integral in L.

Temperature fixed on the left, u(0) = 0; no source (f = 0). A prescribed flux g = -1 on the right end is imposed through the boundary integral int g v ds in the linear form L. The outward flux is q . n = -u'(1) = g = -1, i.e. heat flows in and the slope at the right end is u'(1) = 1. The solution is again u = x, as in example 1, but now the right end value is not prescribed: it follows from the flux.

Summary

The recipe

  1. Write the problem as an operator equation $Au = f$ and determine $O(A) = 2s$ and $m = s - 1$.
  2. Classify the boundary conditions: $c \le m$: Dirichlet, built into the function space (DirichletBC); $c > m$: Neumann, built into the weak form (... * ds).
  3. Multiply by a test function $v$, integrate over $\Omega$, and integrate by parts $s$ times: $a(u, v) = L(v)$.
  4. In FEniCS, write down a and L and call solve(a == L, u, bcs).
The four steps of the lecture. The exercises apply them to the 1D problem of example 2 (with an error norm) and to the fourth-order biharmonic equation of beam bending.

Exercise 1

Analytical solution and error norm for problem 2

Problem 2 reads: $-u'' = 1$ on $(0, 1)$ with $u(0) = 0$ and $u'(1) = 0$.

  1. Derive the analytical solution $u(x)$ (integrate twice, insert the boundary conditions).
  2. Solve the problem with FEniCS (Lagrange elements of degree 1) and compare the nodal values of the FEM solution with $u(x)$ at the nodes. What do you notice? Plot the error $u - u_h$ between the nodes.
  3. Define an error norm, e.g. the $L^2$ norm $\|u - u_h\|_{L^2} = \big(\int_0^1 (u - u_h)^2 \, dx\big)^{1/2}$, and evaluate it with FEniCS (assemble or errornorm).
  4. Increase the polynomial degree of the FEM approximation to 2 and 3 (FunctionSpace(mesh, 'Lagrange', 2)) and evaluate the error norm again. Explain the result.

Exercise 2

Biharmonic equation (theory)

Given is the biharmonic equation in 1D on $\Omega = (0, 1)$ (beam bending: $u$ is the deflection, $f$ the distributed load):

$$A u = \frac{d^4 u}{dx^4} = f .$$
  1. Determine the order $O(A)$ as well as $s$ and $m$.
  2. Set up the integral form $(Au, v) = (f, v)$ and derive the variational formulation by integrating by parts $s$ times. Which boundary terms appear?
  3. According to the criterion $c \le m$ or $c > m$, which boundary conditions are Dirichlet (essential) and which are Neumann (natural) boundary conditions? Which conditions must the test function $v$ satisfy?

Exercise 2, item 4

Galerkin method with cubic Hermite trial functions

We set $f = 0$ and drop the boundary terms. The loading is instead prescribed directly as a load vector. Choose finitely many trial functions $h_i(x)$ and seek $u_h(x) = \sum_i U_i\, h_i(x)$ with unknown coefficients $U_i$. The same functions are used as test functions, $v_h = h_j$. Inserting into the variational formulation from item 2 gives, for each $j$,

$$\sum_i U_i \int_\Omega h_i''\, h_j'' \, dx = \int_\Omega f\, h_j \, dx , \qquad \text{i.e.} \qquad K\, U = F$$

with $K_{ij} = \int_\Omega h_i''\, h_j'' \, dx$ and $F_j = \int_\Omega f\, h_j \, dx$.

Trial functions: the cubic Hermite interpolation functions on $\Omega = (0, 1)$

$$h_1 = 1 - 3x^2 + 2x^3, \quad h_2 = x - 2x^2 + x^3, \quad h_3 = 3x^2 - 2x^3, \quad h_4 = -x^2 + x^3$$

They are constructed such that $U_1 = u_h(0)$, $U_2 = u_h'(0)$, $U_3 = u_h(1)$, $U_4 = u_h'(1)$. Check this using $h_1(0) = 1$ and $h_1'(0) = h_1(1) = h_1'(1) = 0$, and correspondingly for the others.

Exercise 2, item 4

The cantilever beam

The beam is clamped at $x = 0$, $u(0) = 0$ and $u'(0) = 0$; the right end is free. The load vector for the free degrees of freedom $(U_3, U_4) = (u(1), u'(1))$ is

$$\underline{F} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix},$$

i.e. a point force $1$ at the free end and no moment.

  1. Which of the $h_j$ are admissible test functions? Which rows and columns of $K$ are therefore needed?
  2. Compute the required entries $K_{33}$, $K_{34}$, $K_{43}$, $K_{44}$. Why is $K_{34} = K_{43}$?
  3. Solve the system of equations and give $u_h(x)$. Compare with the exact solution of $u'''' = 0$ with $u(0) = u'(0) = 0$, $u''(1) = 0$ and $u'''(1) = -1$.
